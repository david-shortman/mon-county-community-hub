## 2. What worked

- **Volume with almost no breakage.** 674 merges and **0 reverts**. Every Claude review on a merged PR ended at PASS.
- **The review gate caught things and builders filed them.** 28 issues cite a Claude review as their source, and 26 of them are already closed.
- **The trust rules held up once written.** The High stakes check sends shelter, crisis, food and benefits changes to David. David merged 200 PRs labeled `for:david` this week. The weekly spot check confirmed 10 of 10 high-stakes facts.
- **Measuring our own pages against other sources.** The source benchmark gave a clear, honest result (7 of 8 better than every source, 1 of 8 meets the full bar), with the reasons written down.
- **Docs harvest themselves.** The newest reviews (for example `docs/reviews/2026-11-01-simulated-walkthroughs-rerun.md`) end with a list of the PRs and issues they produced. Little is left only in a document.
- **Instant builds from `up-next`, when used, are fast.** Median time from `up-next` to a closed issue: about 13 minutes (19 issues).

## 3. What did not work

- **Findability did not move.** 76 at baseline, 76 on October 31. Our 38 answer pages are not in search yet. Search engine accounts (#89) are still waiting on David, and they are the main thing holding the score back.
- **Scheduled Claude Code runs could not write to GitHub.** The weekly retro failed on October 28 (manual run) and November 2 (scheduled run). Earlier, the first scheduled runs had no repo attached, and a manual "run now" still starts a session without the repo. #1418 addresses the write permission.
- **`for:david` grew faster than David can clear it.** 110 open `for:david` issues and 8 open `for:david` PRs. The oldest (#23 to #26, outreach decisions and the glass question) are about 5.5 days old. Of the 88 open `needs-verification` issues, many are also `for:david`.
- **Three `for:david` PRs were merged by mistake** on October 31 (#712, #787, #906), because the High stakes label arrived a minute after the PR opened (#910). Fixed by `scripts/safe-merge.sh`.
- **`main` broke twice on October 30.** Two PRs that each passed on their own broke the build when they landed together (#313 and #314), and then two sessions sent the same fix (#318, #319). This led to `scripts/pr-wait.sh` and `docs/operations/merge-conflicts.md`.
- **9 PRs that changed `site/` or `content/` merged before their Claude review posted:** #129, #130, #194, #196, #283, #303, #304, #319, #448. All of those reviews passed afterward, but the soft gate says to wait.
- **Scoring stopped after day one.** Of the open `for:code` and `for:duet` issues, only 6 have a `Score:` comment. Almost all issues opened after October 28 have none, so the queue cannot be ranked the way the pipeline says.
- **Claims go stale.** 11 issues are `in-progress` with no update in over 24 hours and no open PR (section 4).
- **Pace.** Budget rules say about 10 to 12 builds a week at normal pace. This week had sprints with hundreds of merges a day. Whether that fit David's usage target is not measurable from here.

## 6. Stop doing

- **Stop merging `site/` and `content/` PRs before the Claude review posts.** Nine did this week. `scripts/safe-merge.sh` waits for the High stakes check, but not for the review.
- **Stop opening issues without a score.** Only 6 open build or Duet issues have a `Score:` comment. Without scores, the plan has to rank by judgment, which nobody else can check.
- **Stop starting scheduled jobs with `fire_trigger` for work that needs the repo.** It starts a session without the repo, and that run cannot do its job.

## 7. Process changes to propose

At most three. Each is its own PR for David, labeled `for:david` and `type:process`, linked in the PR for this retro.

**1. Make `scripts/safe-merge.sh` also wait for the Claude review on `site/` and `content/` PRs (#1434).**
- Problem: the soft review gate depends on each author waiting.
- Evidence: 9 PRs merged before their review this week (#129, #130, #194, #196, #283, #303, #304, #319, #448).
- Change: the script already waits for the High stakes check, and `build` is now a required check (#95). Add the same wait for "Claude review" when the PR changes `site/` or `content/`. This edits a script and the pipeline's review gate section, not repo settings.
- How we will know: the November 16 retro counts 0 PRs merged before their review, and the median time to merge stays under an hour.

**2. A weekly decision batch for `for:david` (#1435).**
- Problem: David's inbox grows faster than he can clear it.
- Evidence: 110 open `for:david` issues, 8 open `for:david` PRs, the oldest about 5.5 days. David merged 200 `for:david` PRs this week on top of that.
- Change: once a week (Duet, before the Monday retro), list every open `for:david` item in one issue, grouped by type (merge a PR, make a decision, make a call), each with a recommended answer and what happens if he says nothing. `needs-verification` issues get `for:david` only when a phone call or contact is the only way left to confirm.
- How we will know: open `for:david` issues fall under 50, and the oldest is under 7 days, by November 16.

**3. A tested way to run a scheduled job now, and a write check at the start (#1436).**
- Problem: scheduled Claude Code runs failed twice: once with no repo, and twice with no permission to write.
- Evidence: day-one runs with no repo; the retro failures on October 28 and November 2.
- Change: in pipeline stage 8 and the operations runbook, write down: "To run a scheduled job now, send the request in its bound session, or move its schedule to a minute from now. Do not use `fire_trigger` for jobs that need the repo. Every scheduled job tries one harmless write first. If that fails, it posts one comment on #81 and stops." Pairs with #1418.
- How we will know: the November 9 and 16 retros run on schedule and merge their own PRs with no hand fix.

**Changes adopted two weeks ago:** none. The project is six days old. The first such check is due on November 16, for: one file per log entry, the High stakes check and `safe-merge.sh` (Oct 30 to 31), and David's November 2 pipeline answers (#1410).
