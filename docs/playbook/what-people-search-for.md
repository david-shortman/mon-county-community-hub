# What people search for, and what they need next

David asked on October 28, 2026: for each situation, what are people on the other end really trying to do? His example: does anyone search "how to get better financially" and expect local resources?

This page pulls together what we could find about what Mon County residents ask for help with, the words they use, and what they need next. It is meant to shape topic landing pages: each page should start from the problem a person arrives with, not from a list of programs.

Last checked: October 29, 2026.

## The short answer

1. **People search for the problem in front of them, not a life goal.** "Help paying electric bill" gets about 14,800 U.S. searches a month. "How to become financial literate" gets about 30. Money pages should be named after the trouble ("can't pay a bill", "behind on rent"), not after self improvement.
2. **Bills and housing are the biggest local needs by far.** In 2025, West Virginia 211 made 63,206 referrals for utility help, 33,968 for food, and 23,178 for housing. Rent and mortgage help was the largest single top need (43%). The last local Mon County 211 breakdown we found (2021) had housing first and utilities second, "far and away".
3. **Most people reach 211 through the web, not the phone.** Of WV 211's 126,728 contacts in 2025, 60,140 were web searches, 18,465 were calls, and 5,842 were texts. So a good web page is not a side door. It is the main door.
4. **Locals ask in plain words and want a name, a price, or hours.** Forum posts say "need help", "recommendations?", "is $1,500 realistic?". They do not use program names. We found no local post asking how to "get better financially". Local money questions were all concrete: what rent costs, what daycare costs, what a job pays, how to cut a phone bill.
5. **Many people will meet us through an AI summary.** In a 2026 survey, 45% of U.S. adults had used an AI tool for local recommendations, up from 6% a year before. When Google shows an AI summary, people click a result about half as often. Pages need a plain direct answer at the top that a summary can quote correctly, with the local name, phone, hours, and a checked date.

## The finance example, in detail

Yes, some people search general phrases like "how to be better with money". But the volume is small, and those searchers want articles and tips, not a local office. People in real money trouble search for a specific bill or debt and want cash help soon:

| What they type (U.S., monthly) | Searches |
|---|---|
| help with utility bills | 22,200 |
| help paying electric bill | 14,800 |
| utility assistance | 14,800 |
| free grant money for bills and personal use | 9,900 |
| debt relief grants | 4,400 |
| i need help paying my electric bill | 1,900 |
| free grants to pay off debt | 1,600 |
| how to become financial literate | 30 |

These are a third party's estimates from Google Ads data (seodata.dev, mid 2026), so treat them as rough. We could not get volumes for local names like "LIEAP" or "Mon Power", or any West Virginia Google Trends data.

Two cautions:

- **"Grants to pay bills" searches draw scam sites.** A money page should say plainly that real help does not charge a fee to apply, and point to known local and state programs.
- **About a third of adults are a few hundred dollars from trouble.** In the Federal Reserve's 2025 survey, 63% could cover a $400 surprise with cash, and 16% did not pay all their bills in full (34% of those earning under $25,000).

What this means for the site: a "Money" landing page should open with situations, each leading to a short path:

- I can't pay a bill this month (utilities, phone, water)
- I'm behind on rent or facing eviction
- I need cash fast and I'm thinking about a payday loan
- I want free help filing taxes (VITA)
- What things cost here (rent ranges, daycare, typical pay)

"Getting better with money" can be a small link at the bottom, pointing to free local counseling if we can source one.

## By topic: what people arrive with and what they need next

Ranked by how strongly the evidence says it matters here.

| Rank | Topic | What they arrive with, in their words | What they need next | Strongest evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bills and utilities | "help paying electric bill", "shutoff notice", "can't pay water" | Which program fits, whether it is open now, what to bring, a number to call today | WV 211 2025 (top referral category); Mon 211 2021 (#2); 9% of local CHNA respondents faced a shutoff threat |
| 2 | Housing and rent | "behind on rent", "eviction", "is $X realistic", "where are listings" | Rent help and legal help today; real local price ranges; where listings actually are; tenant rights | Mon 211 2021 (#1); WV 211 rent and mortgage 43%; Mon Health priority #1; 51% of Morgantown renters are cost burdened (2020) |
| 3 | Food | "food bank near me", "pantry open today", "SNAP" or "food stamps" | Who is open today and when, what to bring, how to apply for SNAP | WV 211 #2 category; 14% of Mon County residents food insecure (Feeding America, 2023 data); about half of WVU students report some food insecurity (2023) |
| 4 | Mental health and substance use | "help today", "therapist taking new patients", "detox" | Someone to talk to now, cost, who takes my insurance, a crisis line | Ruby Memorial CHNA 2025: 64% named mental health and 53% drugs as top problems; 26% could not get care when needed |
| 5 | Cost of care and emergency cash | "free clinic", "can't afford my prescriptions", "dentist no insurance" | Free or sliding fee places, who qualifies, how to get in | CHNA: 50% named cost of care; Fed survey: 26% skipped care due to cost |
| 6 | Moving here and newcomers | "relocating", "which areas", "is $1,500 enough", "who picks up trash at my address" | Neighborhoods in plain terms, real rent ranges, who serves my address (city, county, Cheat Lake), how to set up utilities | Local forum posts (most common topic after recommendations) |
| 7 | Transportation and child care | "bus to the hospital", "average daycare prices" | Routes and times; real local price ranges | CHNA: 21% named transportation; ALICE and health department survey list both as barriers |

What we left out on purpose: the most common forum question was "who's good for X?" (a mechanic, a groomer, a lawn service). Recommending businesses goes against our neutral, common good rule, so the site should not try to answer it.

## How spikes happen

Help searches jump within days of benefit news. During the late 2025 SNAP lapse, one Pennsylvania food bank's locator went from 119 searches a day to 773 in two weeks. National 211 food pantry requests went from about 981 a day to 4,214 on October 29, 2025. People wanted schedules and open hours, not explainers. A page that already exists, with today's hours, is what helps in that moment.

## What this means for every landing page

1. **Title it with the problem, in residents' words.** "Can't pay your electric bill?" not "Energy Assistance Programs".
2. **Answer in the first two lines.** Who to call or where to go, today. This is also what an AI summary will quote.
3. **Then ask one question to sort people.** Which bill? Do you have a notice? City or county? Each answer jumps to a short path (see `docs/design/presentation.md`, question path and step path).
4. **Give local numbers.** Real price ranges, real hours, real phone numbers, with a checked date.
5. **Say what happens next.** What to bring, what the call is like, how long it takes.
6. **Warn about scams where searches draw them** (bill grants, debt relief).

## Gaps and what we could not check

- No West Virginia or Morgantown Google Trends data. Trends pages could not be read from our tools, and we found no published local analysis.
- No search volumes for local names (LIEAP, Mon Power, Mountain Line, MUB).
- The only Mon County 211 breakdown is from 2021. The WV 211 2025 figures come from a news article quoting the report; we did not find the report itself.
- Monongalia County's own ALICE share is in an interactive dashboard we could not reach. Statewide, 45% of WV households are below the ALICE line (17% in poverty plus 28% ALICE).
- Reddit itself blocks automated reading, so we saw post titles only, not full posts. About 60 titles is a small sample, and a topic that did not show up is not proof it is missing. Local Facebook groups were not reachable.
- Our findability questions (`content/findability/questions.yaml`) were written by us, not taken from real searches. This page is a first step toward checking them against what people really ask.
