# Who lives here: audience facts for Monongalia County

This page collects the numbers that tell us who the site has to work for: what phones and internet people have, how well adults read and do math, and the groups most likely to need help. Use it when you design a page, write content, or pick what to build next. The [presentation guide](../design/presentation.md#who-we-design-for) turns these facts into rules for pages.

Last checked: October 28, 2026.

## How to read these numbers

- Most county numbers come from the American Community Survey (ACS) 2024 1-year estimates. For a county this size the margins of error are wide, so treat small differences as rough. Re-check a number on data.census.gov before quoting it on a page residents will read.
- About 1 in 5 residents is a college student. That pulls some numbers around, most of all poverty. Where it matters, this page says so.
- Numbers marked **our estimate** are our own math from published tables. No source states them.

## Phones and internet

Mon County households, out of about 45,000 (ACS 2024 1-year, table S2801):

| Fact | Mon County | West Virginia |
|---|---|---|
| Have a smartphone | 91.2% | 87.2% |
| Smartphone is the only computer | 5.7% | 12.8% |
| Cell data plan is the only internet | 14.2%, about 6,400 households | 12.3% |
| No internet subscription | 7.1% | 10.4% |
| No internet, income under $20,000 | 19.4% | 24.0% |
| No internet, income $75,000 or more | 1.8% | |

- Among people 65 and older, 7.7% have no computer, and another 8.5% have a computer but no internet (ACS 2024 1-year, B28005).
- Western Mon County has the most places with no good broadband. County fiber projects paid with federal ARPA money aim to reach about 2,200 to 2,550 homes and businesses. The statewide BEAD broadband build, for about 73,000 locations, is now expected in 2027 or 2028.
- Nationally, 91% of adults have a smartphone. For incomes under $30,000, 80% do, and 29% use a smartphone with no home broadband (Pew, 2026).
- Phones and data run out. In Pew's last survey on this (2015), 44% of smartphone owners earning under $30,000 had stopped their phone service for a while because of cost, and about half of people who rely on their phone hit their data limit at least sometimes.
- In 2026, 28% of smartphone-only households use prepaid plans, and 55% of them earn under $25,000 (Recon Analytics, an industry study).
- In the U.S. in September 2026, 46% of phones on the web were Android and 54% were iPhone (StatCounter).
- A content site should load in about 3 seconds on a 2020 mid-range Android on slow 4G. That means about 75 KB of script and about 1.3 MB in all (Alex Russell, 2024).
- Adults 50 and older: 90% own a smartphone, texting is their main way to keep in touch, and 3 in 5 say technology is not made with their age in mind (AARP, survey fielded 2025). In 2017, 73% of adults 65 and older said they need someone to help them set up or learn a new device (Pew).

## Money and hardship

- **Poverty:** 19.6% of people (West Virginia 16.7%), 14.0% of children, 14.5% of people 65 and older (ACS 2024 1-year, B17001).
- **Poverty without college students:** about 11.3%. **Our estimate** from ACS 2024 1-year B14006, which shows about 10,300 people in poverty are enrolled in college. Census already leaves out students in dorms, so most of these students live off campus.
- **Median household income:** $66,873 (West Virginia $60,798).
- **SNAP:** about 3,800 households and 7,300 people a month, including about 2,800 children (state fiscal year 2026, West Virginia DoHS).
- **ALICE households** (working but below a basic survival budget, 2023): 67% of single-mother households with children, 49% of single adults, 83% of householders under 25, and 48% of householders 65 and older, as cited in Mon Health's 2025 health needs assessment. West Virginia overall: 45% (2024).
- **Students:** about half of WVU students reported some food insecurity (fall 2023 survey). Intakes at The Rack, the campus pantry, are up 23% in 2025 to 2026.
- **No car:** 8.9% of households, and 16.7% of renter households (ACS 2024 1-year, B25044).
- **Health coverage:** 4.3% uninsured (ACS 2024 1-year, DP03). About 13% are on Medicaid (Data USA, 2024).
- **Paperwork gets in the way.** In late 2025, 10,392 of 28,332 West Virginia applications for Lifeline, the federal phone and internet discount, were turned down because documents were not provided (USAC). That is more than a third.

## Language

- 6.6% of residents age 5 and older speak a language other than English at home, and 1.8% (about 1,800 people) speak English less than very well.
- The most spoken languages after English are Spanish, other Indo-European languages, Arabic, Chinese, Slavic languages, and Vietnamese.
- Source: ACS 2020 to 2024 5-year, C16001. The 1-year table has no county data.

## People in crisis

- Homelessness: the most recent point-in-time count reported about 150 people in the Morgantown area, against about 21 shelter beds (MetroNews, July 2026). The 2024 count was about 100.
- Overdose deaths: 77 in 2023 (West Virginia Office of Drug Control Policy).
- In Mon Health's 2025 community survey, the top health concern was mental health (64%), and the top quality of life concerns were health care costs (50%), poverty (37%), and homelessness (25%).

## What this means for the site

1. **Build for a cheap Android on slow data.** Text first, very little script, and no maps or video loaded unless asked for. Stay inside the weight budget in the presentation guide.
2. **Write simply and do the math.** About 1 in 6 adults reads at the lowest level and 1 in 4 struggles with numbers. One step at a time, plain dates and costs, and big Call, Text, and Directions buttons.
3. **Work for people with disabilities and older adults.** Pages must work with zoom, big text, and screen readers, and never depend on sound alone.
4. **Show the town and how to get there.** Most people live outside Morgantown, and many have no car.
5. **Say what to bring.** Missing papers stop a lot of people, so every service should list the documents it asks for when the source says.
6. **Remember two audiences.** About 1 in 5 residents is a college student, many of them new each year. County averages mix students with long-time residents, so a county number can hide what one group faces.
