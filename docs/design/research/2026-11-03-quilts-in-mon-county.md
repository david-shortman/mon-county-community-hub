# Is there a Mon County quilting tradition? (November 3, 2026)

Monthly identity refresh, research round. The site has used quilt marks since November 2, 2026 (Press). The [sense of place research](2026-11-01-sense-of-place.md) chose them as shared folk geometry and found no barn quilt trail in Monongalia County. This round asked: is quilting tied to this county in a way we can source, or is it only "Appalachian in general"?

**Short answer:** there are real leads, but none is confirmed yet. Until one is, the guide keeps calling quilt marks a shared folk shape, never a local tradition or a barn quilt.

## Risks to weigh before using any of this

- **The relief-era photos tie quilting to poverty relief.** That history is real, but a hub for people in hard times must not suggest "quilts are what poor people here make." As the [Looking at Appalachia](https://bittersoutherner.com/roger-may-looking-at-appalachia) project puts it, the trouble with stereotypes is that they are incomplete. If we ever cite it, pair it with the living guild and keep it in words, never as a photo.
- **Scott's Run is coal history.** The guide avoids coal and mining imagery because of live economic and political debates.
- **A guild or a shop is someone's work.** Never copy a guild's quilt, a shop's pattern or a named designer's block. The marks stay plain, traditional, public-domain geometry.
- **WVU pages.** Mountaineer Week is a WVU event. Citing it as a source is fine; using its look or name in our design is not.
