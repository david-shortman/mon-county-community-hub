# Two official pages differ on whether a credit freeze costs money

- **Found:** 2026-11-01, while building `/identity-theft/` (#222, sprint wave 9, #529)
- **What we found:** USA.gov says a credit freeze is free. The WV Attorney General's identity theft page says freeze costs "vary by state law", that West Virginia victims "may be eligible for free credit freezes with proper documentation", and lists "Pay any required fees as determined by state law" as a step.
- **Evidence:**
  - https://www.usa.gov/credit-freeze (read 2026-11-01): "Freeze or lift the freeze on your credit report for free by contacting each of the three major credit reporting agencies."
  - https://ago.wv.gov/consumer-protection/identity-theft-protection-prevention (read 2026-11-01): "Costs and Requirements: Credit freeze costs vary by state law. In West Virginia, identity theft victims may be eligible for free credit freezes with proper documentation, such as a police report."
- **Why it matters:** a person who reads the state page may think a freeze costs money, or that they need a police report first, and wait. `/identity-theft/` gives USA.gov's rule and the Attorney General's phone number for questions.
- **Who keeps it:** the WV Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division.
- **Status:** open
