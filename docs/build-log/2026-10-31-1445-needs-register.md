# 2026-10-31: The first needs register

- **Considered:** #44 asks for a ranked, sourced list of the problems people in Mon County face. The schema and checker (#551), the API (#719), the care needs ledger (#815) and the County Health Rankings indicators (#778) were already in place, but `content/needs/` was empty. We looked at County Health Rankings, CDC PLACES, both 2025 hospital health needs assessments, the state overdose snapshot, the Kids Count data book, United For ALICE, Map the Meal Gap, HUD rents and news on the point in time count.
- **Chose:** 12 needs, each backed by at least three sources read on October 31, 2026, instead of a longer thin list. A simple score out of 12 (how serious, how many people, how many sources agree, room to help) sets the order, with written tie-breaks. Trends stay "not known yet" unless a source shows change over time; only overdose deaths (77 in 2023, 49 in 2024) have one.
- **Shipped:** `content/needs/` (12 files), a plain `/needs/` page with each need's numbers, sources, local groups and gaps, a link from How is Mon County doing?, a line in llms.txt, and `docs/research/needs-sources.md` (where each source lives, how often it updates, what we could not get). The Mon Health assessment had West Virginia 211's county request counts, which no earlier page had: housing and shelter made up 2,103 of 4,428 requests.
- **Found:** the county has more mental health providers per person than West Virginia or the US, yet residents still name mental health care as the hardest care to get. The page says so plainly instead of calling it a provider shortage.
- **Next:** idea issues for the top gaps (linked from #44). Re-check when County Health Rankings 2026 and the next Kids Count book come out.
- **Cost:** $0. All sources are free and public; no keys or accounts.
