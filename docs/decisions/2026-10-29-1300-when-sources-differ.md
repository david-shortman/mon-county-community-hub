# When trusted sources differ, say who says what

- **Date:** 2026-10-29
- **Decided by:** David, in a Claude Code session (the PR that adds this file waits for his review)
- **Decision:** On health and other sensitive topics, write what a careful person with no special training would reasonably conclude after hearing the main trusted sources. When those sources agree, say it plainly, in one voice. When they differ, name each source and what it says in one line, with no verdict between them. Then point people to their own doctor, pharmacist or the office that does the work, never to a side. Every line links to the group that says it, checked on that group's own page.
- **Why:** In 2025 and 2026 federal vaccine advice (CDC) and doctors' groups (AAP, AAFP, ACOG) began to differ, and public trust is split: in KFF's 2026 polling most adults trust their own doctor, while about half trust the CDC. Picking a side would break the charter's neutrality rule and lose readers who trust the other source. Agreement across sources with different possible biases is the strongest signal a non-expert has, so we lead with it. First used on the flu and COVID shots page (#234).
